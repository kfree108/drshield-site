Every stranding reason is a listing problem: listing closed, listing error, restricted ASIN, under review or deleted. Why 'stranded' gets treated as an inventory problem when it isn't

The word “stranded” and the fact that it shows up on an inventory dashboard lead a lot of sellers to treat it as a supply-chain issue, something to solve with a reorder decision or a shipment plan. It isn't. Every stranding reason traces back to the listing record sitting on top of the inventory, not the inventory itself. The stock is fine; the catalog entry that would let it sell isn't.

Treating a stranding event as an inventory-planning problem means working on the wrong layer. A seller can audit stock counts for weeks and make no progress, because the unit doesn't need to go anywhere. The listing above it needs to be corrected, approved or reinstated, and the inventory can sell from exactly where it already sits once that happens.

The five stranding reasons, and the fix each one needs

Amazon's Stranded Inventory report names a reason for each affected ASIN. In our casework the reasons sort into five, and each needs a different fix:

  • Listing closed: the ASIN was deactivated, most often for a policy or compliance reason attached to the listing. Fix: resolve the underlying issue, then request reactivation.
  • Listing error: a catalog data conflict, frequently introduced by a recent bulk edit, flat-file upload or category change. Fix: find the specific conflicting attribute and correct it.
  • Restricted ASIN: the product now needs category or brand approval, or existing approval documentation has lapsed. Fix: supply the approval or documentation, not a listing edit.
  • Under review: a compliance, authenticity or safety flag is pending on the listing. Fix: answer the review with the evidence it asks for.
  • Deleted: the listing was removed from the catalog, sometimes by accident during a bulk update. Fix: restore or re-create it through the catalog process.

Not one of the five is caused by how much stock you hold or how it was forecast. The Stranded Inventory report is a listing-health report wearing an inventory dashboard's name.

How to find your stranded inventory, step by step

This takes about ten minutes and needs nothing but Seller Central access.

  • Open the report. In Seller Central, go to Inventory and open the Stranded Inventory report (Amazon's help page on stranded inventory explains the layout).
  • Sort by reason. Group the affected ASINs by the stated reason. Five different problems need five different fixes, so one generic resubmission wastes time on most of them.
  • Check recent edits. A reason that appeared right after a bulk upload points to a listing error from that upload. A reason that appeared with no recent changes points more toward a compliance flag or a category-policy shift.
  • Rank by value. Start with the ASINs holding the most units at the highest cost, because storage fees keep accruing while they can't sell.
  • Log the fix and the date. A short record of what was wrong and what cleared it makes the next stranding faster to diagnose.

What to do while the listing is being fixed

Storage fees keep accruing on stranded inventory the entire time it's unsellable, so the fix is time-sensitive. If it will take a long time, for example a compliance issue with a long documentation cycle, weigh whether removing or disposing of that specific inventory makes more financial sense than paying storage on stock that won't sell soon. Make that a deliberate calculation, not a default outcome of the listing never getting prioritized.

Once the listing clears, stage any further changes rather than making every planned edit at once. A listing that just came out of review is better off with a clean, quiet history than with a fresh round of bulk edits.

Where stranded stock sits next to other recoverable money

Stranded inventory is a listing fix, not a reimbursement claim, and it's worth keeping the two apart. Amazon does owe sellers money in other situations where stock goes wrong inside its network. Across the seller accounts we manage, between 10 Feb and 21 Aug 2026 we recovered $803,457 from Amazon, net of $170,047 in reimbursement reversals ($973,504 gross), across 42,096 line items (our own data).

By value, that money came back from these causes, per our master database as of 21 Aug 2026:

  • Customer returns never returned: $508,269 (19,200 items)
  • Lost inbound: $215,676 (1,271 items)
  • Customer service issues: $102,858 (4,202 items)
  • Damaged in warehouse: $83,426 (5,594 items)
  • Lost in warehouse: $62,818 (5,233 items)

Stranded units don't appear in that list because they aren't lost or damaged. They're intact and waiting on a listing. If a unit is both stranded and later found damaged or lost, it is then a separate reimbursement case.

Which one you should actually pick

If stranded inventory shows up on your dashboard, resist the instinct to treat it as a stock problem. Every one of the five reasons is a listing issue, and the fastest way to stop the storage fees is fixing the catalog record the report points to. A seller with a small catalog can work through the report weekly alone. A seller with a large catalog and frequent bulk edits will get more from a connected, contingency-priced service like Shield.

Common questions

What causes stranded inventory on Amazon?

A listing-level issue: listing closed, listing error, restricted ASIN, under review or deleted. It isn't caused by inventory quantity or forecasting.

Do I still pay storage fees on stranded inventory?

Yes. Storage fees are charged on the space inventory occupies in Amazon's warehouse whether or not the listing above it is sellable, so stranded inventory accrues storage as if it were active.

How do I find out why my inventory is stranded?

Open the Stranded Inventory report in Seller Central. It names the specific reason for each affected ASIN, so check it directly rather than guessing from how the listing looks.

How do I fix stranded inventory on Amazon?

Fix the listing according to the stated reason: resolve the compliance issue for a closed or under-review listing, correct the conflicting data for a listing error, supply documentation for a restricted ASIN, and restore a deleted listing. Once the listing is active, the stock can sell again where it sits.

Is stranded inventory the same as lost or damaged inventory?

No. Stranded units are physically intact and waiting on a listing fix. Lost or damaged units are a separate reimbursement matter.

Should I remove stranded inventory instead of fixing the listing?

Sometimes, if the fix is far off and ongoing storage costs outweigh the inventory's value. Make it a deliberate decision after assessing the fix timeline, not a default that happens because the listing issue never got prioritized.
