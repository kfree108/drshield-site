What Amazon account health actually is

Account Health is a page in Seller Central. It is Amazon's running report card on how you sell: whether orders arrive on time, whether customers are happy, and whether your listings and conduct follow Amazon's policies. It is split into a few areas, and each one can raise a warning on its own.

  • Customer service performance: order defects, cancellations, late shipments and returns handling.
  • Policy compliance: listing violations, product authenticity complaints, intellectual property complaints and restricted-product flags.
  • Shipping performance: on-time delivery and valid tracking, which matter most for sellers who ship their own orders.
  • Account Health Rating (AHR): a single score that summarises the above. Amazon describes 200 and above as the healthy band.

Amazon sets a target for each individual metric and changes them from time to time, so read the current numbers on your own dashboard instead of trusting a figure copied from a blog post, including this one.

Why it matters: it sits behind almost every deactivation

Most sellers look at the dashboard only when something has already gone wrong. That is late. A poor rating, repeated policy flags or an unanswered notice are among the main routes to a suppressed listing and, at the far end, a deactivated account. The ground cited in a notice, not the label (deactivated or suspended), decides what you can do next, so read it before you reply.

The useful way to think about it: Account Health is the early-warning system and the appeal file is the late one. Everything you fix while the status is still green costs you an afternoon. The same issue fixed after a deactivation costs you sales every day the account is dark.

A weekly checklist that takes 15 minutes

The pages that rank for this topic mostly describe the dashboard. What they leave out is a routine. This is the one we would hand a seller with a small team.

  • Open Account Health once a week, on the same day, and note the AHR and any metric shown as at risk or poor.
  • Clear policy flags first. A flagged listing or a complaint with a response deadline outranks a slow-moving metric, because deadlines are what turn a warning into an action against the account.
  • Check the notification inbox and the registered email, not just the dashboard. Amazon's outreach can land in either.
  • Confirm your emergency contact number and email are current. A stale contact detail is one of the quietest ways to miss a notice.
  • Name a backup person who can see the account, so a holiday or illness does not leave the inbox unwatched.
  • Write down what you changed and when. If a case ever opens, a dated record of fixes is the evidence you will want.

The part most guides skip: listing health is account health

Account Health is usually described as a customer-service scorecard. In practice listing problems feed it and cost money before they ever reach a notice. In the accounts we manage, every reason Amazon gives for stranded inventory, meaning stock that sits in its warehouses but cannot be sold, is a listing problem: listing closed, listing error, restricted ASIN, under review or deleted. You can see your own in the Stranded Inventory report in Seller Central.

That is the practical reason to treat the dashboard as more than a rating. A restricted ASIN or a closed listing is a policy and listing issue first and an inventory and cash problem second. Catching it in Account Health, before the units are stuck, is cheaper than recovering from it afterwards.

Account Health Assurance: the protection layer on top

Amazon runs a free programme called Account Health Assurance (AHA). For an eligible seller, it keeps the account active through most policy-violation deactivation triggers as long as the seller engages with Amazon's account health team within 72 hours and completes the corrective action. Amazon's criteria include a Professional selling plan, an AHR of 250 or above for six months with no more than 10 days below that line, and a valid emergency contact.

Note that 250 is stricter than the 200 healthy band. AHA is for accounts that are comfortably healthy, not merely adequate, and it is not blanket immunity. Check Amazon's current AHA page for the full conditions.

When to handle it yourself and when to get help

Most account health work is routine and a seller can do it directly: fix a listing, respond to a customer issue, keep contact details current. A small account with one person who checks the dashboard every week usually does not need outside help.

Help earns its place when a case is account-level, when a notice cites a policy ground you cannot reproduce from your own records, or when nobody on the team is watching the inbox daily. Common questions

What is Amazon account health?

It is the Seller Central dashboard that shows how your account is performing against Amazon's customer service, policy and shipping standards, along with an Account Health Rating that summarises it. Amazon describes a rating of 200 or more as healthy.

Where do I find my Amazon account health?

In Seller Central, open the Performance menu and choose Account Health. The page lists each area, its current status and any open policy flags.

What is a good Account Health Rating?

Amazon describes 200 and above as the healthy band. For Account Health Assurance, the programme's condition is 250 or above held for six months, with no more than 10 days below that line.

How do I improve my Amazon account health?

Clear open policy flags and anything with a response deadline first, then work on the metrics shown as at risk, such as late shipments, cancellations and order defects. Check the dashboard weekly so problems are small when you find them.

Can a low account health get my account deactivated?

Yes. A poor Account Health Rating is one of the grounds Amazon can cite, alongside policy breaches and other conduct. The sooner a falling metric is fixed, the less likely it reaches that point.
