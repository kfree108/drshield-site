The short answer

Seller Performance (now largely surfaced through Account Health) tracks the metrics and policy compliance that determine whether Amazon lets you keep selling: order defect rate, policy compliance, and, for many accounts, a Voice of the Customer section flagging quality trends before they become violations. Reading it regularly, not only after a notice arrives, is what turns it into an early-warning system rather than a scorecard you check reactively.

What's actually inside this dashboard

The Seller Performance area brings together several distinct signals under one roof: your account health rating, the specific metrics feeding order defect rate, a policy compliance section listing any active violations or warnings, and — for accounts it applies to — a Voice of the Customer section surfacing product-quality signals (return reasons, review sentiment on specific ASINs) that haven't yet triggered a formal violation but indicate a trend worth addressing. Most sellers only open this dashboard after receiving a notice; the more useful habit is treating it as a standing check, the way you'd check inventory levels or ad spend.

The specific sections available can vary somewhat by account and marketplace, so it's worth a few minutes familiarizing yourself with exactly what your own dashboard currently shows rather than assuming it matches what a generic guide describes.

Bookmark the specific page rather than navigating to it fresh each time — a small friction-reduction that makes the weekly habit easier to actually keep.

Familiarity here pays for itself the first time it matters.

Reading the account health rating correctly

The headline rating is a summary, not the full picture — a seller can sit at a seemingly healthy overall rating while one specific metric or policy area is trending toward a threshold. Drill into the specific components rather than checking only the top-level number: is order defect rate stable or climbing over the recent window, are there any active policy warnings even if they haven't triggered suspension, and does Voice of the Customer show any ASIN trending toward a quality flag. The overall rating lags these specifics, which means it's the last place a developing problem shows up, not the first.

It also helps to note the trend, not just the current snapshot — a metric that's stable but elevated is a different situation from one that's climbing steadily even from a currently acceptable level, and the dashboard's historical view, where available, is worth checking specifically for that trajectory.

A five-minute weekly scan of these specific components, rather than the single summary number, is the entire habit worth building here — nothing more elaborate is required to catch most developing issues early.

A worked example of catching an issue early

A seller checking Seller Performance weekly notices Voice of the Customer flagging one ASIN for an increase in "item defective" return reasons over the past two weeks — well before any formal violation has been issued. Investigating immediately reveals a packaging change from a supplier that's causing minor damage in transit. The fix — reverting the packaging and confirming with the supplier — happens before the trend ever escalates into a return-policy or authenticity-adjacent violation. A seller who doesn't check the dashboard until a formal notice arrives faces the same root cause, but discovered weeks later, after more customers have been affected and after Amazon's systems have already begun formal action.

The gap between these two outcomes — a quiet packaging fix versus a formal violation — is entirely a function of when the underlying signal was noticed, not how severe the underlying issue actually was. The issue was identical in both cases; only the discovery timing differed.

Small, boring habits like this one rarely feel urgent in the moment, which is exactly why they're easy to skip and exactly why skipping them is expensive.

What to do when a metric or flag first appears

The moment a metric shows meaningful movement or a new flag appears — even one that hasn't triggered a formal notice — is the cheapest point to investigate it. Pull the specific orders or ASINs behind the flag, identify the actual mechanism, and address it the same way you would a formal violation, just before Amazon's own threshold forces the issue. Treating an early Voice of the Customer flag with the same diagnostic rigor as a suspension notice, rather than waiting to see if it escalates, is consistently the highest-leverage habit available on this dashboard.

This same discipline extends to positive signals worth tracking too — noticing a metric trending in the right direction after a fix confirms the fix actually worked, which is useful information in its own right and worth a brief note for future reference if a similar issue ever resurfaces.

Speed of response, once a signal appears, matters more than the sophistication of the response itself.

The common mistake: checking only when forced to

The recurring pattern we see is a dashboard checked only reactively — after an email notification forces the visit — rather than on a standing schedule. By the time a formal notice arrives, the underlying issue has usually been building for a period the dashboard's own earlier signals would have shown. Building a weekly or biweekly check into a standard operating rhythm, the same way inventory or advertising metrics get checked, closes this gap at essentially no cost beyond the habit itself.

Setting a recurring calendar reminder, tied to a specific day each week or two, is a low-effort way to make this a genuine habit rather than an intention that quietly lapses once the current crisis passes.

The accounts that struggle most with this dashboard are rarely the ones with the worst underlying operations — they're the ones that simply never developed the habit of looking until something forced the issue, which is a genuinely fixable gap with almost no cost to close.

Small, consistent habits beat large, occasional efforts here.

Seller Performance is most useful as an early-warning system, not a scorecard checked after the fact — the specific metrics and Voice of the Customer trends inside it usually show a developing problem well before a formal notice does. Common questions

How often should I check Seller Performance?

Weekly or biweekly as a standing habit, not only when a notification forces a visit — early trends in Voice of the Customer or individual metrics are visible well before they escalate into a formal violation.

What is Voice of the Customer on Amazon?

A section surfacing product-quality signals — return reasons, review sentiment — at the ASIN level, often before those signals have triggered a formal policy violation, making it one of the earliest warning indicators available.

Does a healthy overall account health rating mean nothing is wrong?

Not necessarily — the overall rating is a lagging summary, and a specific metric or policy area can be trending toward a threshold while the headline rating still looks acceptable. Check the specific components, not just the summary number.

Can I fix an issue flagged by Voice of the Customer before it becomes a violation?

Often yes — because these flags typically surface before formal enforcement, investigating and addressing the underlying cause at this stage frequently prevents the issue from escalating into a violation at all.
