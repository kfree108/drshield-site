How Much a Suppressed Amazon Listing Costs You Per Day

Updated November 4, 2026 · By Ken Freeman · 1750 words · Written against what currently ranked for “how much a suppressed Amazon listing costs you per day”
The short answer

A suppressed Amazon listing costs you its average daily sales, plus any ad spend still running and storage on stock that cannot sell. For example, a SKU selling 20 units a day at $28 loses about $560 a day in gross sales. Your own velocity sets the real figure.

The daily cost formula in one line

Use this for any suppressed ASIN: daily cost = (units per day x average selling price) + daily ad spend still running on the ASIN + daily storage on the stock behind it. Then add a recovery tail, because sales rarely snap back to baseline on the day the listing returns.

  • Units per day: use a 30-day average from your Business Reports, not your best week.
  • Ad spend still running: check every campaign that targets the ASIN, including auto campaigns and product targeting. Paid clicks cost the same whether or not the listing can convert.
  • Storage: Seller Central's fee preview shows what the stock behind the ASIN costs to hold. The figure is small per day but it never stops.
  • Recovery tail: the days after the fix when sales run below normal while rank rebuilds. Estimate it from your own history if you have a past stock-out or suppression to compare against.

None of these inputs is a market average. They all come from your own account, which is why no article can give you a single honest dollar figure per day.

Why this number is worth calculating even roughly

Most sellers never put an actual figure on what a suppression is costing, because the listing-quality dashboard tells you a status, not a dollar amount, and nobody is forcing the arithmetic. A problem with no attached number tends to get deprioritized behind anything that does have one, even when the suppression is genuinely more urgent. Running even a rough version of the calculation turns an invisible cost into a comparable one, which is often what gets a fix moved to the top of the queue.

The two components of the daily cost

Lost sales is the obvious one: a search-suppressed listing does not appear in organic search, which for most ASINs is where the majority of unpaid traffic and a meaningful share of paid traffic conversion comes from. A suppressed listing can still receive direct-link traffic, but the discovery channel that drives most volume is closed. Amazon's own help pages on suppressed listings are the place to confirm which kind of suppression you have, since the exact effect on search and the Buy Box depends on the reason.

The second component gets missed more often: inventory sitting behind a suppressed listing keeps accruing monthly storage fees exactly as if it were sellable, so the daily cost is foregone revenue plus an ongoing carrying cost on stock that is not moving.

A worked calculation

For example, take a SKU that normally sells 20 units a day at a $28 average selling price: $560 a day in gross sales (20 x $28). Suppressed for a week, that is roughly $3,920 in lost gross revenue (7 x $560).

Now add ads. If the same SKU had $40 a day of campaign spend still pointed at it, that is another $280 over the week (7 x $40) spent sending shoppers to a listing that cannot sell. The week's combined cost in this example is about $4,200 before storage and before any recovery tail.

Then the storage side: if that SKU holds 30 days of cover in Amazon's warehouse, none of that inventory turns over during the week, so the carrying cost sits on top with nothing offsetting it. The revenue side dwarfs storage for most SKUs, but storage is the part that keeps compounding after the fix, until the backlog sells through. These are illustrative numbers, not a benchmark. Why the real cost is usually worse than the calculation

The arithmetic above assumes sales resume at the exact same rate the moment suppression clears, and that is optimistic. Organic ranking for a suppressed listing does not necessarily hold its prior position once visibility returns. A listing absent from search for a week or two can come back lower than it left, because ranking systems weight recent sales velocity and a suppressed listing generated none. So the true cost is the suppressed days multiplied by daily sales, plus a recovery period afterward where sales run below the old baseline while rank rebuilds.

If ad spend kept running against the ASIN the whole time, the true daily cost also includes that wasted spend, as in the worked example. A paid click landing on a listing that cannot convert returns nothing, which is worse than pausing the campaign for the duration.

Rank your suppressed ASINs by daily cost, not by who noticed first

When several listings are affected at once, the order of the fix queue matters as much as the speed. Here is an example with three suppressed ASINs, using made-up velocities:

  • ASIN A: 40 units a day x $15 = $600 a day.
  • ASIN B: 5 units a day x $60 = $300 a day.
  • ASIN C: 12 units a day x $25 = $300 a day.

Fix A first, even if B is the one a colleague complained about. Two days on A costs about $1,200 in gross sales (2 x $600), the same as four days on B (4 x $300). A bestseller suppressed for two days can cost more in absolute dollars than a slow SKU suppressed for two weeks, purely on velocity.

Add a column for ad spend still running on each ASIN and a column for how many days of stock sit behind it. The ASIN with the highest total, not the loudest complaint, goes to the top.

What to do while you calculate

Do not wait for a precise number before starting the diagnosis. Every day spent calculating is another day the cost accrues, so run both in parallel:

  • Pause ads on the suppressed ASIN right away. Paying for clicks to a listing shoppers cannot find or buy through search is waste on top of lost organic sales.
  • Read the suppression reason in Seller Central's listing-quality tools and fix what it names, whether that is a missing attribute, an image problem or something else.
  • Check for stranded stock. Open Seller Central's Stranded Inventory report. Every stranding reason is a listing problem (listing closed, listing error, restricted ASIN, under review, deleted), so stranded units and a suppressed listing often share a cause.
  • Plan the recovery window. Once the fix is live, watch sales velocity for the following one to two weeks rather than assuming full recovery on day one, and plan inventory and ad spend around that lag.

Which one you should actually pick

There is no universal dollar figure for what a suppressed listing costs. It depends on the SKU's own velocity, the ads still running and the stock in storage. What is universal is the shape: the cost builds daily and does not stop the day suppression clears, because rank takes time to rebuild. Run the calculation once on your highest-velocity ASIN, rank your affected listings by daily cost, and treat listing health as a daily check, not a monthly one.

Common questions

How do I calculate the daily cost of a suppressed listing?

Multiply average daily units by average selling price for that ASIN, add the ad spend still running on it, then add the storage fees accruing on stock that cannot sell. For example, 20 units x $28 = $560 a day before ads and storage. Use your own 30-day averages.

Does a suppressed listing lose its organic rank permanently?

Not permanently, but rank often does not hold its prior position the moment suppression clears. Ranking systems weight recent sales velocity and a suppressed listing generates none, so expect a recovery period with below-baseline sales after the fix.

Should I pause ads on a suppressed listing?

Generally yes. If the listing cannot appear in organic search or cannot be purchased, paying for ad clicks to it is largely wasted spend layered on top of the lost organic sales. Restart once the listing is live again and check conversion before raising bids.

Does a suppressed listing still cost money in storage fees?

Yes. Inventory behind a suppressed listing keeps accruing standard monthly storage fees in Amazon's warehouse, exactly as if the listing were sellable, because the physical stock has not moved. Your fee preview in Seller Central shows the amount for your stock.

How do I find suppressed listings and stranded inventory in my account?

Seller Central's listing-quality and Fix Your Products tools show suppressed listings and the reason for each. The Stranded Inventory report shows units sitting in fulfillment centers with no active listing, and every stranding reason it gives is a listing problem. Check both at least weekly.
