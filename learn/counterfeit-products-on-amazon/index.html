What counts as a counterfeit on Amazon

A counterfeit is a product that copies a brand's trademark, name or packaging without the brand owner's permission, so a buyer believes they are getting the genuine item. Amazon's anti-counterfeiting policy covers exactly this, and it applies to everyone who sells in its store, whether they sell their own products or someone else's.

It helps to separate counterfeits from three things that look similar from the outside:

  • Gray market goods: genuine products sold outside the brand's intended channel. They are real, but the seller may not be authorized.
  • Used or refurbished items listed as new: a condition problem, handled differently from a fake.
  • Look-alike products: similar designs sold under a different name. These raise design or patent questions rather than a counterfeit claim, unless they use the brand's mark.

The label matters because each one is reported in a different way, and a report filed under the wrong heading is slower to resolve.

How shoppers can spot a fake product

There is no single giveaway, so use several checks together. None of them proves a product is fake on its own, but a listing that fails two or three deserves a closer look.

  • Check who is selling it. On the product page, look at the line that says who ships and who sells. A seller you have never heard of, with a new storefront and few ratings, deserves more care than the brand's own store or Amazon itself.
  • Compare the price. A price far below the brand's own website is a reason to pause. Genuine discounts exist, but a deep gap on a premium item is worth questioning.
  • Read the listing closely. Blurry images, mismatched model numbers, spelling errors and missing brand details are common signs.
  • Read the reviews for what they describe. Look for buyers reporting different packaging, a different smell or texture, or a product that does not match the photos.
  • Inspect the box on arrival. Compare the packaging, labels, batch codes and seals with the brand's official product page. Check expiry dates on anything consumable.
  • Stop using it if something is off. For items you ingest, apply to skin, charge or use around children, safety comes before returns.

If you decide a product is fake, you can return it through Amazon's normal process and report it to Amazon. Customers who think they received a counterfeit can contact Amazon customer service directly.

How brand owners report counterfeit products

Brand owners have a faster route than ordinary customers. Enrolling a trademark in Amazon Brand Registry gives access to reporting tools that let you flag listings that infringe your marks and send in evidence about them. A clean report usually contains:

  • the listing's ASIN and the seller's storefront name
  • your trademark registration details
  • a clear explanation of why the item is not genuine, such as different packaging, a missing security feature or a manufacturing detail you can point to
  • photos or a test purchase showing the problem, where you have one

Specific, well-organized evidence is what moves a report forward. A report that says only that a listing looks fake gives the reviewer nothing to compare. A report that places genuine and suspect packaging side by side, with the exact difference marked, gives them something to act on.

Amazon decides every report on its own facts, and no one outside Amazon can promise how a given case will turn out. What a brand can control is the quality of what it sends and how consistently it follows up.

Why counterfeit problems often hide inside other numbers

Most pages on this topic stop at spotting and reporting. The part they miss is that, for a brand, counterfeits rarely show up as a line item labelled counterfeits. They show up as symptoms in the seller account:

  • A weaker Buy Box position. Across 393 ASINs on 19 brands we manage, over 30 days, the brand did not hold the Buy Box in 24.9% of snapshots. Counterfeit or unauthorized sellers are only one possible cause, since pricing and stock levels also play a part. But a brand that sees this should check who else is winning the box on its listings.
  • Unexplained complaints. Buyers who receive a fake often leave reviews or returns that describe the product, not the seller. A cluster of comments about a quality issue you cannot reproduce in your own stock is a prompt to investigate.
  • Listing and account health flags. Complaints about authenticity can feed into account health. Catching the pattern early is easier than explaining it later.

The practical step is a regular review: check the sellers on each of your top listings, buy a sample from any seller you do not recognize, and keep the evidence organized by ASIN. A short, repeatable routine finds more than an occasional deep dive.

A brand protection checklist you can run this week

  • Confirm Brand Registry is active and that your trademarks are registered in the marketplaces where you sell.
  • List your authorized sellers and keep it somewhere your team can reach, so you can tell quickly who is on a listing without permission.
  • Check your top ASINs for other sellers once a week, and note any new names.
  • Order a test buy from sellers who are new, unusually cheap or unfamiliar, and photograph the packaging against your own.
  • Keep reference material: photos of genuine packaging, batch code formats, invoices from your manufacturer and your trademark documents.
  • Log every report with its case ID, date and evidence, and reply inside the open case instead of starting a new one.
  • Read the reviews for authenticity complaints and group them by ASIN.

Reviews that break Amazon's community guidelines can also be reported, and we tell brands straight when a review is simply negative and cannot be removed. A fake-product complaint from a real buyer should be treated as information about your listing, not as something to suppress.

Common questions

How do I know if a product on Amazon is counterfeit?

Check the seller, compare the price with the brand's own site, read the listing for errors and review the packaging on arrival against the brand's official product page. No single sign is proof, but a product that fails several checks is worth reporting.

How do I report counterfeit products on Amazon?

Customers can contact Amazon customer service and report the listing from the product page. Brand owners enrolled in Brand Registry can report infringement directly, with the ASIN, trademark details and evidence that the item is not genuine.

Is Amazon responsible for counterfeit products sold by third parties?

Amazon's anti-counterfeiting policy prohibits counterfeit goods across its store, and it investigates reports. Questions about legal responsibility in a particular case depend on the facts, so a brand with a serious problem should speak to a lawyer.

What should I do if I already used a fake product?

Stop using it, particularly if it is a supplement, cosmetic, battery or children's product. Keep the packaging, contact Amazon for a return or refund and report the listing. If you have a health concern, speak to a medical professional.

Can a brand get a negative review removed because it mentions a fake?

Only reviews that break Amazon's community guidelines can be reported for removal. A real buyer describing a counterfeit they received is useful information about your listing, and the better response is to find and report the seller.
