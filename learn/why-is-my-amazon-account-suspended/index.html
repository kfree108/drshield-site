Why Is My Amazon Account Suspended? Diagnosing the Actual Cause

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1408 words · Written against what currently ranked for “why is my amazon account suspended”
The short answer

Amazon suspends accounts for five broad reasons: a performance metric crossing its threshold (order defect rate, late shipment), a policy violation (listing accuracy, prohibited content), an authenticity complaint, an intellectual property claim, or a related-account link. The notice itself names which one — read it carefully before assuming, because each of the five requires a genuinely different response to clear.

Read the notice carefully before you guess at anything

The instinct when a suspension hits is to search for a generic fix, but the notice you received almost always names the specific reason, and that reason determines everything about how you respond. A performance-metric suspension needs a plan addressing the metric and its underlying cause. An authenticity complaint needs supplier invoices. An intellectual property notice needs a response to the specific rights owner's claim, sometimes direct contact with them. Treating all five causes with the same generic, downloaded plan of action template is the single most common, and most avoidable, reason a first appeal fails — it answers a question the reviewer isn't asking.

It's worth reading the notice twice before acting on it: the first read for the general shock of the news, the second read specifically hunting for the ASINs, order IDs, dates, and named policy or metric it cites. Those specifics are usually there, even in notices that read as vague on a fast first pass.

The five categories, and what triggers each

Each category behaves differently, which is why identifying the right one before acting saves time later — a metric issue is usually visible in advance, while an authenticity or intellectual property notice tends to arrive with no warning at all. Performance metrics — order defect rate, late shipment rate, or a similar account health measure crossing Amazon's threshold, usually visible on your Account Health dashboard before the suspension itself lands. Policy violations — listing accuracy, prohibited content, or a specific selling policy breach, typically flagged on a specific ASIN or set of ASINs. Authenticity complaints — a customer or rights holder reporting a suspected counterfeit or inauthentic item, which shifts the burden to proving your supply chain. Intellectual property claims — a rights owner formally reporting infringement, which routes through a different appeal built around responding to that specific claim. Related-account links — Amazon connecting your account to another suspended or previously terminated account through shared information like a bank account, address, or device — which needs evidence disproving or explaining the connection, not a standard plan of action at all.

A worked example of correct diagnosis

A seller receives a suspension notice referencing "customer complaints about item condition" on a specific ASIN. That phrasing sits closest to a policy violation or, depending on volume, a performance-metric issue tied to order defect rate — not an authenticity complaint, even though "item condition" might sound similar. Pulling the actual order history for the flagged ASIN shows twelve returns over three weeks citing damaged packaging, which points to a fulfillment or packaging root cause, not a sourcing one. The correct response addresses packaging and fulfillment corrective action; a response built around supplier invoices — appropriate for an authenticity case — would miss the actual underlying issue entirely and would likely get rejected for not addressing what was flagged.

The twelve-return detail matters beyond confirming the cause — it also tells you the scope of the corrective action needed. A fix that addresses one order but not the packaging process behind all twelve reads as incomplete to a reviewer checking whether the underlying pattern, not just one instance of it, has actually been resolved.

What's actually at stake while it's unresolved

A suspended account isn't just paused — it's actively and measurably losing ground on the metrics that determine how it performs once reinstated. Across a 30-day snapshot of 393 ASINs on 19 managed brands, the brand did not hold the Buy Box in 24.9% of snapshots — and a suspended listing holds it in zero. Every day a suspension runs is a day competitors, where they exist, can take that share, and a day of lost sales history and organic ranking momentum that doesn't fully return the moment the account is reinstated. That's the argument for diagnosing correctly the first time rather than filing quickly and hoping.

None of this means rushing the response — it means being efficient with the time you do spend, by pointing that time at the actual cause from the start rather than a guessed one that produces a rejection and a second, avoidable round of the same delay.

What to do when the notice itself is vague

Some notices genuinely underspecify the cause — a general reference to "a violation of our policies" without naming the specific policy or ASIN. In that case, check Account Health for any related flags first, since the notice and the dashboard sometimes surface different levels of detail for the same underlying issue. If that's still unclear, the plan of action or appeal submission itself can include a factual request for clarification on which specific instances triggered the action, alongside whatever corrective steps you can reasonably take across the likely causes — better than guessing at one cause and writing a plan that misses the real one entirely.

Cross-referencing helps too: pull your last 30 to 60 days of order and listing activity and look for anything that changed around the time the notice arrived — a new supplier, a content edit, an unusual spike in returns or complaints. The notice's timing is itself a clue when its language isn't specific, because whatever triggered it usually happened in a fairly narrow window before the notice landed.

Which one you should actually pick

Common questions

How do I find out exactly why my Amazon account was suspended?

Read the specific notice in full and check Account Health for related flags — Amazon generally names the category (performance metric, policy violation, authenticity, IP, or related account) even when it doesn't spell out every detail.

Can my account be suspended without warning?

Some violations trigger immediate suspension, particularly authenticity and intellectual property complaints, while performance-metric issues are usually visible on Account Health before they escalate — checking that dashboard regularly catches metric-based risk earlier.

What's the difference between a policy violation and an authenticity complaint?

A policy violation is about listing content, conduct, or a specific selling rule. An authenticity complaint is specifically about whether the product itself is genuine, and it requires supplier documentation rather than a standard corrective-action plan.

Can a related account cause my own account to be suspended?

Yes. Amazon links accounts through shared information like banking details, addresses, or devices, and a suspension on one linked account can trigger review or suspension on another — this needs evidence addressing the connection itself, not a standard plan of action.

How do I check my account health before it leads to a suspension?

The Account Health dashboard in Seller Central shows current standing against order defect rate, policy compliance, and other tracked metrics — reviewing it regularly, not only after a notice arrives, catches metric-based risk early enough to fix before it triggers suspension.
