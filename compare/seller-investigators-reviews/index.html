What we could and could not verify

We checked Trustpilot and G2 directly for a rating specific to Seller Investigators, read 21 August 2026. Neither returned a product-level score. Trustpilot indexes reviews at the Carbon6/SPS Commerce company level, spanning a suite of separately built tools far beyond reimbursement — an aggregate figure there would describe the whole portfolio, not this product, so we print no number rather than borrow one that does not actually apply. G2's listing landscape for adjacent Threecolts products in this category shows the same pattern — a "Seller Bench FBA Reimbursements" listing exists with zero reviews, for a different vendor entirely, which illustrates how thin product-level review data runs across this whole niche rather than being unique to Seller Investigators.

"No reviews" is a finding, not a gap in our research

When a specific product carries no independently verifiable review corpus, the honest response is to say so plainly rather than substitute a related number that looks close enough. A company-wide Trustpilot score answers a different question than "is this specific reimbursement tool good," and printing it under this product's name would be a worse error than printing nothing. What that leaves you to evaluate instead: the vendor's own published terms, the scope of what it claims to cover, and the parent company's stability — all of which are genuinely checkable even without a star rating.

What Seller Investigators actually offers

Its own product page, read 21 August 2026, states: "we take a 25% commission only after you've been paid" — no upfront fees, standard contingency structure for the category. Coverage spans five published claim categories including customer returns and overage/dimension discrepancies, which is genuine breadth relative to some narrower competitors. The product sits inside SPS Commerce, a public EDI and supply-chain data company that acquired Carbon6 for approximately $210 million, closing 4 February 2025 — real financial backing, even without a public product review to confirm service quality against it.

The 25% rate matches GETIDA's published floor and Refunds Manager's stated rate almost exactly, which places Seller Investigators squarely in the mainstream of this category's pricing rather than at either extreme — TrueOps' 10% undercuts all three, and none of the 25% vendors, this one included, is the cheapest option available if price is the deciding factor.

How to actually diligence a vendor with no review corpus

Ask for reference accounts directly — a vendor confident in its service should be willing to connect you with two or three current clients rather than pointing at a rating that does not exist. Ask specifically how claims are audited before filing, since Amazon's own policy penalizes "insufficiently researched or premature requests" with "delayed support on their cases or be subject to monitoring, investigation, and account action" — a vendor's internal audit discipline matters more here than it would for a vendor with a thousand reviews vouching for it. And ask the change-of-control question directly, given the recent acquisition: what happens to your rate and your account data if SPS Commerce restructures or sells the reimbursement line again.

Why aggregate company scores would mislead here specifically

Carbon6, before and after the SPS Commerce acquisition, has assembled several separately built Amazon seller tools under one brand — recovery, inventory, advertising, chargeback products among them. A company-wide review score averaged across all of them tells you almost nothing about the reimbursement product specifically: a strong review of the advertising tool and a weak one of the recovery product cancel into a number that describes neither accurately. That is precisely why we print no rating here rather than reach for the nearest available number — an inaccurate figure attached to the wrong product is worse than an honest gap, and the reader deserves to know which one they are getting.

The scope this product does not cover

Like every reimbursement specialist in this category, Seller Investigators is scoped to money recovery — lost, damaged and mishandled inventory claims. It does not offer listing reinstatement, variation-family protection, Brand Registry casework, or account-health defense. A brand evaluating it purely on the strength of its parent company's size should keep that scope boundary in view; SPS Commerce's scale backs the reimbursement product specifically, not a broader casework capability it does not claim to offer.

The claim windows that apply regardless of which vendor you use

Whatever recovery vendor eventually files on your behalf, the same Amazon rules govern the outcome. Customer-return claims are eligible no sooner than 60 days and no later than 120 days after the refund or replacement; fulfillment-center operations claims must be filed within 60 days of the item being reported lost or damaged; removals in transit run 15 to 75 days from shipment creation. Pre-order losses are reimbursed at sourcing cost rather than sale price since 31 March 2025, and Amazon caps single-unit reimbursements at $5,000. A vendor's rate matters less than whether its filing discipline respects these windows — ask directly how median time-to-filing compares against them, for Seller Investigators or any competitor.
