What Threecolts actually is

Threecolts is a holding company for Amazon and e-commerce seller software, built substantially through acquisition. Its reimbursement product, DimeTyd Sellers, is the merged remains of three previously independent brands: Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper. All three now redirect into one property, with the footer reading "3CG 2025 SellerBench LLC." Separately, its inventory and accounting suite, Seller 365, is the current name for what was previously InventoryLab. FeedbackWhiz, a review and feedback tool, lives inside that same suite rather than as its own standalone product.

The same pattern shows up elsewhere in this category: Carbon6 was acquired by SPS Commerce, and Downstream was absorbed into Jungle Scout Cobalt. Threecolts has simply brought more brand names under one structure than most.

The rating, and exactly what it does and does not cover

Trustpilot lists a TrustScore of 4.4 from 67 reviews for threecolts.com, read 21 August 2026. This is a company-wide figure. It spans reimbursement recovery, inventory management, feedback tools and whatever else sits under the Threecolts umbrella. It is not a rating of DimeTyd Sellers, Seller 365 or any single product.

A buyer of one specific tool should treat this number as background on the parent company, not as evidence about the product they are about to purchase. Sixty-seven reviews spread across a whole suite is also a modest sample, so it supports a limited conclusion at most.

Why the consolidation happened: Amazon changed the reimbursement pool

Three reimbursement brands folding into one is a visible symptom of a wider shift. According to Amazon's own reimbursement policy, proactive reimbursement for inventory lost in fulfillment centers has been automated since November 2024, pre-order losses are valued at sourcing cost rather than sale price since 31 March 2025, and claim windows now run 60 to 120 days rather than the old 18 months.

When the easiest claims are automated and the windows tighten, fewer standalone brands can sustain themselves on contingency fees for a smaller pool. Threecolts' roll-up is a checkable example of that pressure, and it says nothing negative about the quality of any one tool.

What DimeTyd Sellers offers, on its own current terms

Read from dimetydsellers.com, 21 August 2026: a weekly invoice for 25% of what is recovered, covering more than 50 reimbursement scenarios, with a dedicated case manager per account and an FBA Fee Protector add-on that compares SKU measurements against Amazon's fee report daily. That fee-protection add-on is the closest any Threecolts product comes to dimension-dispute work, and it is a genuine strength worth crediting independently of the company-wide rating.

For a seller whose main need is a hands-off reimbursement filer with a published rate, DimeTyd Sellers is a legitimate option, and a published percentage is ahead of us on transparency.

Where the money is: what recoveries look like in practice

Whichever vendor you pick, it helps to know what claims are worth chasing. Across the seller accounts we manage, between 10 February and 21 August 2026, we recovered $803,457 net from Amazon: $973,504 gross, less $170,047 in reimbursement reversals, across 42,096 line items. This is our own figure, from the Full Circle master database, and it is not a benchmark for any other vendor.

By value, the largest category in that same period was customer returns never returned: $508,269 across 19,200 items. Lost inbound inventory was next at $215,676 across 1,271 items. If a vendor's scenario list is thin on customer returns and inbound shipment discrepancies, ask how they handle them.

Fee overcharges are a separate lane from reimbursement. In one case from our own accounts, a unit weighing 1.119 lb in a 6.77 x 6.38 x 6.06 in box was billed at 2.00 lb, because the box sets the fee, not the product. Dimension checks like this are what a fee-monitoring add-on exists to catch.

What a contingency percentage means in dollars

A worked example, for illustration only: if a tool recovered the $803,457 net figure above and charged 25% of recoveries, the fee would be about $200,864, and the seller would keep the rest. Percentages of recovery are common in this category and perfectly workable. What matters is the basis the percentage is applied to, so ask whether it applies to gross recovery or to recovery net of any reversals Amazon later issues, and whether the fee is returned if a reimbursement is reversed.

The claim rules that apply to any Threecolts product

Whichever product files a claim, the same Amazon rules set the boundary. Single-unit reimbursements are capped at $5,000. Claim windows run 60 to 120 days for customer returns and 60 days for most fulfillment-center issues. Amazon also reserves the right to reverse a reimbursement issued in error. Amazon's reimbursement policy warns that "insufficiently researched or premature requests, or submitting a large number of requests in a short time" can trigger "monitoring, investigation, and account action" which makes careful, evidence-backed filing more important than filing volume.

A diligence checklist for a roll-up vendor

A company built through acquisition raises questions a single-product vendor does not. Reasonable asks of any vendor, including us:

  • Change of control: if the product line you use is sold or merged again, what happens to your rate, your data and your support contact?
  • Written notice of fee changes: how much notice do you get, and in writing?
  • Data portability: can you export your claim history if you leave?
  • Reversal handling: is the fee refunded when Amazon reverses a reimbursement?
  • Review dates: is the review you are reading about the current product or one of the absorbed brands?

The last point matters here in particular. Older reviews of Seller Locker, SellerBench, Refund Sniper or InventoryLab describe products under earlier ownership. SellerBench's G2 listing, for instance, carried zero reviews when we checked, a specific and checkable gap. Date any review before weighing it, and confirm which tool it describes.

DimeTyd Sellers is a legitimate, currently priced reimbursement option with real fee-dispute depth through its Fee Protector add-on. Judge it on its own current terms, not the company-wide rating or an absorbed brand's old reviews, and ask the change-of-control question. What to do with this

Judge this on the job you need done, not the feature list. Ask how they check a claim before filing it so Amazon does not reverse it later, and what they do when a case is closed without an answer. A service that only files claims is a different purchase from one that argues them to the end.

Common questions

Is Threecolts' 4.4-star rating specific to any one product?

No. It is a company-wide Trustpilot score (4.4 from 67 reviews, read 21 August 2026) covering the entire suite, not a single tool like DimeTyd Sellers or Seller 365. Treat it as background about the parent company.

What brands has Threecolts absorbed?

At least four in this space: Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper merged into DimeTyd Sellers, and InventoryLab became Seller 365, which also now houses FeedbackWhiz.

How much does DimeTyd Sellers charge?

Its own site, read 21 August 2026, describes a weekly invoice for 25% of what it recovers. Check the current page before signing, since pricing can change.

Does DimeTyd Sellers cover fee and dimension disputes?

Yes, through its FBA Fee Protector add-on, which compares SKU measurements against Amazon's fee report daily. That addresses a recurring overcharge rather than a one-time reimbursement claim.

Is Seller 365 the same as InventoryLab?

Seller 365 is the current name for what was previously InventoryLab. If you are reading older InventoryLab reviews or documentation, confirm it against current Seller 365 material, since features or pricing may have changed.

What should I ask a vendor that has been through acquisitions?

Ask what happens to your rate, data and support contact if your product is sold or merged again, how fee changes are notified in writing, and whether you can export your claim history. These are fair questions for any vendor, including us.
