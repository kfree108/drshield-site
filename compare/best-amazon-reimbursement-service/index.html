Five vendors, one basic model, and rates that actually differ

Every reimbursement specialist in this category runs the same commercial shape: no subscription, a percentage of what is recovered, billed when the money lands. Read from each vendor's own page on 21 August 2026: GETIDA publishes "performance-based pricing starting at 25%" with the first $400 recovered free; Seller Investigators (Carbon6, now inside SPS Commerce) takes "a 25% commission only after you've been paid"; Refunds Manager charges "25% of whatever reimbursements we recover," billed only after the money is confirmed deposited, and explicitly excludes cases Amazon auto-reimbursed without a claim being filed; DimeTyd Sellers (Threecolts' merged Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper) invoices weekly for "25% of what we recover"; and TrueOps undercuts all four at "industry-leading 10% commissions." TrueOps genuinely is the cheapest published rate in the category — any claim otherwise, including from us, would be false.

What separates them beyond the number

Refunds Manager's billing design is worth naming specifically: it only takes a percentage of cases its own team filed, so a seller is never billed on reimbursements Amazon issued automatically — a distinction that matters more since Amazon's November 2024 rollout of proactive auto-reimbursement for lost-in-fulfillment-center inventory. DimeTyd Sellers is the closest any competitor comes to fee-dispute work through its FBA Fee Protector add-on, which compares SKU measurements against Amazon's fee report daily. GETIDA's scale and Selling Partner Appstore listing are real advantages for a seller who wants the most-recognized name. Seller Investigators sits inside SPS Commerce, an enterprise EDI company, which suits brands straddling 1P wholesale and 3P recovery under one corporate roof. None of the five covers suppressed listings, variation protection, or Brand Registry casework.

The change most rankings for this keyword never mention

Reimbursement is a category being reshaped by Amazon's own rules, and almost no ranking article says so. Amazon's updated FBA reimbursement policy, effective 31 March 2025, shifted valuation from selling price to sourcing cost for pre-order losses — a materially smaller number per unit. Claim windows tightened: customer-return claims run 60 to 120 days after the refund, fulfillment-center issues within 60 days; the old 18-month window is dead. And since 1 November 2024, Amazon proactively reimburses inventory lost in its own warehouses without a claim being filed at all — the single easiest category a recovery vendor used to find has been automated away.

Three of the historical brand names in this space — Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper — have already consolidated into one Threecolts product. Carbon6 sold to SPS Commerce. Refully went dark entirely. Pure-reimbursement contingency revenue is a shrinking pool, and the unrecovered money has not disappeared — it has moved into categories none of these five vendors are built to touch: fee and dimension disputes, suppressed listings, variation wrecks, Brand Registry hijacks, and account-health cases.

What that shift looks like inside a real book of accounts

Across our own managed accounts since 10 February 2026, reimbursement recovery totals $803,457 net of $170,047 in reversals, across 53 accounts and 42,096 line items — customer returns never returned account for the largest share at $508,269 across 19,200 items, followed by lost inbound at $215,676. In the same set of accounts, $286,642 of inventory sat stranded in Amazon's warehouses, unsellable, and 393 tracked ASINs across 19 brands lost the Buy Box in 24.9% of snapshots over 30 days — neither of which a reimbursement-only vendor is built to see, let alone fix.

How to choose, by what you actually need

If reimbursement recovery is the whole problem and price is the deciding factor, TrueOps at 10% is the cheapest published rate we found, with a strong G2 record (4.9 from 81 reviews) for its size. If you want the biggest, most-recognized name with an Amazon Appstore listing, GETIDA is the rational default. If your account straddles 1P and 3P, Seller Investigators' place inside SPS Commerce is a genuine fit. If fee-dispute monitoring alongside recovery matters, DimeTyd Sellers' Fee Protector add-on is the closest a pure-reimbursement vendor gets to that work. None of these is the wrong answer for a seller who only needs money back.

If the account is also losing the Buy Box, sitting on suppressed listings, fighting a variation attack, or carrying an account-health risk alongside the reimbursement opportunity, a single-purpose recovery vendor answers one question out of five. That is the case for systematic case management instead.

A short checklist before you sign with any of them

Whichever vendor you choose, five questions are worth asking before you connect an account, and every one applies to us as much as to the five above. How do you separate recoveries you caused from reimbursements Amazon issued automatically, and how is that reflected on the invoice? Which claim categories are covered, specifically — lost and damaged units, inbound discrepancies, removal orders, customer-return mismatches, fee-level errors — since few vendors cover all of them equally well. What is your median time from detection to filing, given that claim windows are now counted in days rather than months. Do you touch fee and dimension disputes, which are the largest recurring leak most reimbursement-only vendors never open. And what happens to your rate and your data if the company is acquired — ownership in this category moves often, and change-of-control terms are ordinary diligence, not paranoia.

Common questions

Which Amazon reimbursement service is cheapest?

TrueOps, at "industry-leading 10% commissions" published on its own site, read 21 August 2026 — half the rate of GETIDA, Seller Investigators, Refunds Manager and DimeTyd Sellers, all around 25%. It is a fair, verifiable claim and worth stating plainly rather than around.

Why did my reimbursement recovery estimate drop compared to last year?

Amazon's FBA reimbursement policy, effective 31 March 2025, shifted pre-order loss valuation from selling price to sourcing cost — a smaller number per unit for most sellers. Claim windows also tightened to 60 to 120 days. Any estimate based on older policy assumptions will read too high.

Does a reimbursement service recover money for lost warehouse inventory automatically?

Since 1 November 2024, Amazon proactively reimburses inventory lost in its own fulfillment centers without a claim being filed. A reputable reimbursement vendor should not bill you for those cases — Refunds Manager explicitly says so on its FAQ page, and it is a fair question to ask any vendor, including us.

Do reimbursement services cover fee disputes or suppressed listings?

Almost never. DimeTyd Sellers' Fee Protector add-on is the closest any of the five vendors above comes to fee-dispute work, and none covers suppressed listings, variation protection, or Brand Registry casework — that gap is what case-management products like Dr. Shield are built to close.

What is the honest ceiling on a pure reimbursement service?

It only recovers money Amazon already owes for lost, damaged or mishandled inventory — and that pool has shrunk under Amazon's own 2024–2025 policy changes. It cannot touch a suppressed listing, a mispriced dimensional-weight fee that repeats every shipment, or a Buy Box lost to a competitor, all of which routinely cost an account more than an unaudited reimbursement claim.

What happened to Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper?

All three now redirect into DimeTyd Sellers, Threecolts' consolidated reimbursement product — the domains 301-redirect and the underlying entity is the same. Historical reviews and pricing pages for those three names describe a product that no longer exists on its own; treat any listicle still ranking them as a standalone option as stale.
