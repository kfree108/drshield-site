What happened to Seller Locker

Checked 21 August 2026: sellerlocker.com 301-redirects to dimetydsellers.com. Seller Locker was one of three previously independent reimbursement brands — alongside SellerBench and Refund Sniper — that Threecolts has consolidated into one product. There is no standalone Seller Locker pricing page, signup, or support channel anymore; any listicle still ranking it as an independent option is describing a company that no longer exists in that form.

The specialty that actually survived the merger

This is the detail worth knowing that most "Seller Locker reviews" articles do not mention: Seller Locker's fee-dispute DNA lives on specifically as DimeTyd Sellers' FBA Fee Protector add-on, which compares SKU measurements against Amazon's own fee report daily and requests corrections when they diverge. That is genuinely rare in this category — of every reimbursement vendor we researched, the merged DimeTyd Sellers product is the only one that comes close to correcting a recurring fee error rather than filing a one-time reimbursement claim.

Why a dimension error is worth more attention than a single reimbursement claim

A single reimbursement claim recovers money once. A mispriced dimensional-weight fee repeats on every unit shipped until someone disputes the recorded measurement — a materially different kind of leak. A real case from our own casework illustrates the shape of the problem: a unit weighing 1.119 lb, shipped in a 6.77 by 6.38 by 6.06 inch box, was billed by Amazon at 2.00 lb. That is not a one-off error; it is a standing overcharge that compounds with every shipment until the recorded dimensions are formally disputed. This is exactly the category of problem the old Seller Locker brand specialized in, and it is worth evaluating the merged product specifically on how well it still does that job, not just on its general reimbursement rate.

What a genuinely engineered fix looks like, beyond disputing the fee

A recurring dimensional-weight overcharge is not always best solved by disputing the recorded measurement — sometimes the more durable fix is changing the box itself. In one case from our own casework, keeping a product's total packaged volume under roughly 243 cubic inches was worth an estimated $1.94 to $2.56 per unit by dropping it into the next fee band down; on that product's best-selling variant, the fee had been leaving roughly 20% margin against 44 to 53% on sibling products, and the entire gap traced back to packaging rather than pricing. Fulfillment fees are, more often than sellers expect, a packaging-engineering problem rather than a negotiation or dispute problem — worth knowing before assuming a fee-dispute service alone solves the whole issue.

The claim windows and caps that apply regardless of which vendor files

Whichever vendor handles a fee dispute or reimbursement claim in your account, Amazon's own rules set the outer limits. Single-unit reimbursements are capped at $5,000. Pre-order losses are reimbursed at sourcing cost — your documented manufacturer or wholesaler cost, not the selling price — since 31 March 2025. Submissions can be declined outright for outlier values or documentation that is "forged, tampered with, or illegible." And Amazon reserves the right to reverse a reimbursement already paid if it was issued in error or the item is later found. None of that changes based on which brand name is filing the claim, old or merged.

What to ask before assuming Seller Locker's old fee-dispute focus still applies

If fee and dimension disputes were the reason Seller Locker appealed to you specifically, confirm directly with DimeTyd Sellers whether the FBA Fee Protector add-on is included in the base reimbursement service or billed separately, and ask how frequently it actually re-checks measurements — "daily" is the published claim, worth verifying against your own account's activity log once connected. That specificity matters more than the general 25% headline rate, since fee-dispute work is the one thing this merged product does that most competitors in this file do not offer at all.

How a dimension dispute differs from a reimbursement claim, in practice

The two are handled through entirely different mechanisms inside Amazon's own systems, and conflating them is a common mistake. A reimbursement claim asserts that a specific unit was lost, damaged, or mishandled and asks Amazon to pay for that specific unit — a one-time transaction tied to a case ID. A dimension dispute asks Amazon to re-measure a product and correct its recorded size tier going forward — a standing correction, not a per-unit claim. One useful caution worth knowing before requesting a re-measurement on any product: a re-measurement corrects errors in both directions, and in our own casework a client once asked us to dispute what they assumed was an overcharge, only for the audit to find Amazon was actually under-charging on nine of their products. The commercially right call was to leave those nine alone and fix only the ones genuinely wrong through packaging changes — knowing when not to file is a real part of fee work that almost nothing published about Amazon fees mentions.

Common questions

Does Seller Locker still exist?

Not independently. sellerlocker.com now redirects to dimetydsellers.com, Threecolts' consolidated reimbursement product, which also absorbed SellerBench and Refund Sniper.

What was Seller Locker known for specifically?

Fee and dimension-dispute detection — comparing an account's actual product measurements against what Amazon billed. That specialty survives inside DimeTyd Sellers as the FBA Fee Protector add-on.

Is a dimensional-weight overcharge worth more attention than a single reimbursement claim?

Often, yes — a reimbursement claim recovers money once, while a mispriced dimensional-weight fee repeats on every unit shipped until the recorded measurement is formally disputed, making it a compounding leak rather than a one-time event.
