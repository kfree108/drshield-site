The rate, read from the page most reviews skip

Refunds Manager's homepage does not carry a pricing table, which is why several rankings for this keyword state that no rate is published. Its own FAQ page tells a different story: app.refundsmanager.com/FAQ.html, read 21 August 2026, states plainly: "We charge 25% of whatever reimbursements we recover. We only bill for a reimbursement once we've confirmed it's been deposited in your account, or the inventory has been replaced." The rate is published — it simply is not on the marketing homepage, which is a real lesson in where to actually look before concluding a vendor discloses nothing.

The billing clause that deserves real credit

The same FAQ page adds a second sentence that matters more than the headline rate: "We only take a percentage of cases for which our team provided the return (so you are not billed for auto-reimbursements Amazon makes)." Since Amazon began proactively reimbursing lost-in-fulfillment-center inventory in November 2024 without a claim being filed, this distinction is not a technicality — it is the difference between paying a vendor for work it did and paying a percentage on money Amazon was always going to send you. Very few competitors in this category state the exclusion this explicitly, and it is worth asking any reimbursement vendor, including us, to match it.

The review record, and why it is the strongest one in this category

Trustpilot lists a TrustScore of 4.9 from 389 reviews for Refunds Manager, read 21 August 2026 — matching the figure the company advertises on its own homepage exactly, which is itself a useful check: the third-party score and the self-reported one agree. Among every reimbursement specialist we researched for this brand, this is the largest and strongest public review corpus by a wide margin — GETIDA carries 7 reviews, TrueOps 81, and Refunds Manager nearly five times TrueOps' count at a materially higher score. That volume, sustained at 4.9, is a genuinely rare combination in this niche.

Refunds Manager's homepage also advertises figures like "recover up to 95%," "$800M recovered" and a "99% success rate." Those are the company's own self-reported claims rather than independently verified figures, and should be read as marketing rather than fact — the Trustpilot record is the part of this review that is actually third-party verified.

The pivot toward 1P vendor recovery, and what it means for a 3P seller

Refunds Manager is increasingly leading with 1P vendor recovery — shortages, co-op fees, chargebacks for brands selling wholesale to Amazon Vendor Central — while continuing to run its original FBA seller service. If your business sells exclusively 3P through Seller Central, the core reimbursement service is unaffected by that positioning shift. If you straddle both 1P and 3P, the fact that one vendor covers both sides under a strong review record is a genuine convenience worth weighing, similar to the argument for Seller Investigators sitting inside SPS Commerce.

How Refunds Manager's figure compares to a real book of accounts

Refunds Manager's own homepage advertises up to 95% recovery rates and a $800M lifetime total — self-reported figures, not independently audited. A more useful comparison point is what a transparent, net-of-reversals figure actually looks like in practice. Across our own managed accounts since 10 February 2026, reimbursement recovery totals $803,457 net of $170,047 in reversals across 53 accounts — roughly 17% of the gross figure clawed back by Amazon's own reversal right. Any vendor's headline recovery number, Refunds Manager's included, is more trustworthy stated net than gross, and it is a fair question to ask any recovery specialist how their own published totals are calculated.

What the review record does not tell you

A 4.9-star average across 389 reviews is strong evidence that Refunds Manager delivers on its stated job reliably. It says nothing about anything outside that job. Like every reimbursement specialist in this category, Refunds Manager does not touch suppressed listings, dimension or fee disputes beyond the reimbursement claim itself, variation and Brand Registry attacks, or account-health cases. A seller judging vendor quality purely by review score risks assuming a strong recovery specialist is also covering ground it was never built to cover.

A lesson worth generalizing: check more than the homepage

The reason several rankings for this keyword state Refunds Manager publishes no rate is that they likely only read the homepage, which carries no pricing table. The actual number lives on the FAQ page, one click deeper. That is a useful habit for evaluating any vendor in this category, not just this one: a marketing homepage is written to convert, and pricing or policy detail often lives on a support, FAQ, or terms page that ranks lower in a casual read. Before concluding any vendor "doesn't publish" something, check the support and legal pages specifically, not just the page a search result lands on.

Refunds Manager suits a seller who wants reimbursement recovery with the strongest verified review record in the category and a billing policy that genuinely protects against paying for Amazon's own auto-reimbursements — a rational, well-evidenced choice for that specific job, and one we would not talk a reader out of. For an account that also needs fee disputes, listing casework or Brand Registry protection, that is outside Refunds Manager's scope entirely, and it is where Dr. Shield's broader casework is built to work instead.

Common questions

How much does Refunds Manager charge?

25% of whatever reimbursements it recovers, per the company's own FAQ page, billed only once the reimbursement is confirmed deposited in your account or the inventory is replaced. The rate does not appear on the marketing homepage, but it is published on the FAQ page.

Does Refunds Manager charge for reimbursements Amazon issues automatically?

No — its FAQ page states explicitly that it only takes a percentage of cases its own team filed, so you are not billed for Amazon's automatic reimbursements. That distinction has mattered more since Amazon's November 2024 rollout of proactive auto-reimbursement.

Is Refunds Manager's 4.9-star Trustpilot rating trustworthy?

It is the strongest, largest public review record we found in this category — 389 reviews at a 4.9 average, read 21 August 2026, and it matches the figure the company advertises on its own site. It is a genuinely strong signal, though it reflects reimbursement work specifically, not any broader casework.

Does Refunds Manager handle 1P Vendor Central recovery too?

Yes — it has increasingly pivoted toward 1P vendor recovery (shortages, co-op fees, chargebacks) alongside its original 3P FBA seller service, which suits a brand that sells both wholesale and through Seller Central under one vendor.

Does Refunds Manager cover fee disputes or Brand Registry cases?

No — like the rest of the reimbursement category, it is scoped to money Amazon already owes for lost, damaged or mishandled inventory. Fee disputes, suppressed listings, variation protection and Brand Registry casework sit outside its mandate.

Where is Refunds Manager's pricing actually published?

On its FAQ page, not its homepage — app.refundsmanager.com/FAQ.html states the 25% rate and the billing policy plainly. Several rankings for this keyword report no published rate because they likely only checked the marketing homepage.

Is Refunds Manager's "$800M recovered" figure independently verified?

No — it is a self-reported figure on the company's own homepage, not an independently audited total. Treat it, along with the "up to 95%" and "99% success rate" claims on the same page, as marketing rather than verified fact. The Trustpilot record is the part of this review that is genuinely third-party verified.

Does Refunds Manager's rate change based on account size?

The published FAQ states a flat 25% with no mention of volume-based tiers, unlike GETIDA's "starting at" phrasing which implies negotiation room. Confirm directly whether any volume discount is available for larger accounts, since the published wording alone does not rule it out.
