What happened, and why the site does not say so

SPS Commerce, a public EDI and supply-chain data company, announced the acquisition of Carbon6 Technologies on 2 January 2025 at a total purchase price of approximately $210 million, roughly 40% payable in SPS stock, and closed the deal on 4 February 2025 — confirmed in SPS Commerce's own press release, mirrored in an SEC 8-K exhibit. Read carbon6.io itself today, and none of that appears. The site still presents Carbon6 as an independent brand with its own suite of Amazon seller tools — recovery, inventory, advertising, analytics and chargeback products under one banner.

This is not unusual in this category; several vendors we track (Perpetua via Flywheel/Omnicom, Sunken Stone rebranded as Emplicit, Downstream absorbed into Jungle Scout Cobalt) trade under a legacy name with no ownership disclosure on the live site. Worth knowing before you sign a contract with any of them: the company issuing your invoice may not be the company whose name is on the page.

The two-product confusion buyers actually hit

Carbon6's own 3P Amazon reimbursement product, Seller Investigators, still lives on carbon6.io and publishes its own pricing: "we take a 25% commission only after you've been paid," read 21 August 2026, with no upfront fees. Separately, SPS Commerce runs its own "Revenue Recovery" product for retailer deductions and distributor shortages — a 1P/wholesale EDI concern, not FBA seller reimbursement. That page does not mention Seller Investigators or Carbon6, and Seller Investigators does not mention SPS. Two products, two audiences, one parent company, and neither page tells you the other exists. If your business straddles 1P wholesale and 3P Amazon selling, you likely need both, from the same corporate roof, under two different names.

Why an aggregate Carbon6 rating tells you almost nothing

Carbon6 is not one product — it assembled several separately built Amazon seller tools under one brand before the SPS acquisition, spanning recovery, inventory, advertising and chargeback categories. An overall company-level rating averages across all of them, which means a strong review of the advertising tool and a weak review of the reimbursement product cancel each other into a number that describes neither. If you are evaluating Seller Investigators specifically, look for reviews naming that product, not the Carbon6 umbrella — and be aware that at product level, third-party review platforms frequently have no listing at all for it, which is itself worth noting rather than treated as silence.

What Seller Investigators is genuinely good at, and its real limits

Its published claim-category breadth is real — five categories including customer returns and overage/dimension discrepancies — and it now sits inside an enterprise EDI company with real staying power, which matters if you weigh vendor longevity heavily. That is a genuine strength for a brand that wants a large, financially stable parent behind its reimbursement vendor. What it does not do: listing reinstatement, variation control, Brand Registry casework, or account-health defense. It is money-recovery only, like the rest of the reimbursement category, and post-acquisition naming makes it harder than most to even find the right page to evaluate.

What reimbursement recovery looks like in practice: a worked check

Whatever vendor you pick, the question that matters is what actually comes back and what it costs you. Across the seller accounts we manage, our master database shows $803,457 recovered from Amazon between 10 February and 21 August 2026, stated net of $170,047 in reversals from a gross of $973,504, over 42,096 line items. Those are our own figures from our own accounts, not a Carbon6 benchmark, and not a promise of what your account will return.

The mix is the useful part, because it tells you which claim types to ask any vendor about. By value, the largest category in that same dataset was customer returns never returned ($508,269 across 19,200 items), followed by lost inbound shipments ($215,676), customer service issues ($102,858), damaged in warehouse ($83,426) and lost in warehouse ($62,818).

For example, if a contingency vendor takes 25% of a $10,000 recovery, you keep $7,500. Ask whether the percentage applies to the gross claim or the net after Amazon reverses part of it later, because reversals are common enough that we state our own total net.

How to actually evaluate Seller Investigators today

Names often outlive a change of owner in this space: Sunken Stone is now Emplicit and ChannelAdvisor is now Rithum, so a review can describe a company that no longer exists in that form. Four checks cut through the confusion. Before anything else, ask who currently owns the business, what happens to your data and rate if it is acquired again, and whether your contract has a change-of-control notice period, which is a fair question for any vendor including us. Next, confirm you are reading carbon6.io/seller-investigators specifically, not a general Carbon6 or SPS Commerce marketing page — pricing and scope live on that one URL. Second, if your business has any 1P wholesale or retailer-deduction exposure, ask explicitly whether SPS Commerce's separate Revenue Recovery product is relevant to you, since neither page will volunteer the connection. Third, treat any third-party review or rating you find as suspect unless it clearly names the specific product — an aggregate Carbon6 score blending advertising, inventory and recovery tools tells you nothing usable about the reimbursement product specifically.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you need done, not the feature list. Ask how they check a claim before filing it so Amazon does not reverse it later, and what they do when a case is closed without an answer. A service that only files claims is a different purchase from one that argues them to the end.

Common questions

Who owns Carbon6 now?

SPS Commerce, a public supply-chain and EDI data company, completed its acquisition of Carbon6 Technologies on 4 February 2025 for approximately $210 million, roughly 40% in SPS stock. Carbon6.io still operates under its own name and does not disclose the ownership change on the pages we read.

Is Seller Investigators the same as SPS Commerce's Revenue Recovery?

No. Seller Investigators is Carbon6's 3P Amazon FBA reimbursement product, still hosted on carbon6.io. SPS Commerce's own Revenue Recovery product is a separate, 1P/wholesale-focused deduction-recovery tool. Neither page mentions the other, and buyers straddling both sides of the business need to know they are two different products.

How much does Seller Investigators charge?

"We take a 25% commission only after you've been paid," per carbon6.io/seller-investigators, read 21 August 2026 — no upfront fees, standard contingency pricing for the category.

Is Carbon6 still a legitimate company after the SPS Commerce deal?

Yes. SPS Commerce is a public company and its own press release confirms the acquisition closed on 4 February 2025. Carbon6.io continues to operate and Seller Investigators is still sold there. The issue is disclosure and naming, not legitimacy: the live site does not mention the new owner.

Does Carbon6 or Seller Investigators handle suppressed listings or Brand Registry cases?

No — Seller Investigators is scoped to FBA reimbursement recovery. Listing reinstatement, variation protection and Brand Registry casework sit outside its mandate.
