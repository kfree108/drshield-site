"Reinstatement" covers at least four different jobs

Every ranking article for this keyword lists the same six or seven names and scores them against each other as if they compete for the same case. They mostly do not. Reinstatement work splits into shapes that require different skills, and matching the shape to the case matters more than any vendor's marketing.

  • Law firms. Amazon Sellers Attorney (James E. Ranalli, Seattle) and Amazon Sellers Lawyer (the trading name of Rosenbaum & Segall, P.C.) are actual law firms — attorney-client privilege, demand letters, and the ability to take Amazon into arbitration under the Business Solutions Agreement when the case-file route is exhausted. Both advertise flat-fee pricing with no dollar amount published on either site, as of 21 August 2026.
  • Human consulting firms. Riverbend Consulting is the closest thing to a pure specialist here — suspensions, ASIN reactivations and account-health protection plans, staffed largely by people who worked at Amazon. It quotes on a call after reviewing the case rather than publishing a rate card.
  • Bundled account-management retainers. eGrowth Partners sells reinstatement work alongside broader Seller Central management — virtual-assistant coverage, PPC management, listing optimization — with reinstatement as one line among several. Its own site states pricing "depends on the complexity of your case," quoted after a review, with no published rate card.
  • Systematic case management. This is where Dr. Shield sits — reinstatement, reimbursement, fee disputes and Brand Registry casework run as one product with a published method, rather than a single specialist engagement.

A naming collision worth knowing before you search

Search the exact keyword and you will land on at least three different companies with confusingly similar names, which is itself the most useful thing we can tell a reader here. Amazon Sellers Lawyer (amazonsellerslawyer.com) is the trading name of Rosenbaum & Segall, P.C., a law firm run by managing attorney CJ Rosenbaum, handling suspension appeals, arbitration and IP defense. Amazon Sellers Attorney (amazonsellersattorney.com) is a separate firm — James E. Ranalli, based in Seattle. A third, AMZ Sellers Attorney (amazonsellers.attorney), is a third distinct entity again. None of the three is a rebrand of another; they are three separate businesses that happen to have picked overlapping names in the same niche.

This matters because reviews, complaints and testimonials for one frequently get attributed to another in comment threads and forum posts — a reader comparing star ratings across search results is often comparing three unrelated companies without realizing it. Before you act on any review you read for this keyword, confirm which exact domain it is reviewing.

The craft that actually moves a case, more than the title on the door

What separates a reinstatement that lands from one that drags is less about credentials than about case discipline, and it is rarely written down anywhere a seller would look. Amazon support has privately confirmed to us that replying to your own open appeal just to chase it can restart the review from the beginning — a mechanism that is not documented on any public Amazon page. The practices that follow from that are specific: keep exactly one case identifier rather than opening a second appeal in frustration, request an internal review rather than re-filing (a fresh appeal restarts the clock; an internal review does not), call every three days and make the representative document the call in the case notes, and never send a stand-alone reminder into an open appeal.

On one case in our own book, four suppressed listings for a children's brand took 40 to 45 days of escalations and appeals to reinstate — and the recovery afterward had to be staged one change at a time: titles first, then item highlights, then secondary images, with an observation period between each step. Sellers routinely undo a hard-won reinstatement in the first 48 hours by fixing everything on the listing at once, which reads to Amazon's systems as a second event worth re-flagging.

This is the case-craft Dr. Shield runs on every open case: parallel tracks (an account-health case, a support case and a specialist email opened together, not sequentially), the three-day call cadence, one case ID, and internal review chosen over re-appeal by default. None of it is exotic. Almost none of it is published by anyone else in this category.

What none of them can promise, and why that is the honest answer

Amazon decides reinstatement cases. Every service on this page — the law firms, the consultants, the retainer shops, and Dr. Shield — argues a case; none of us approves it. Any vendor implying otherwise is selling something it cannot deliver. Amazon's own reimbursement policy page states plainly that sellers who submit "insufficiently researched or premature requests, or submitting a large number of requests in a short time" may face "delayed support on their cases or be subject to monitoring, investigation, and account action" — which is Amazon's own description of what a bad case-filing pattern does to an account, and the reason a rushed, duplicate-happy approach to reinstatement is a bad trade at any price.

What genuinely differs between vendors is scope and cadence, not whether an outcome can be guaranteed. A law firm's leverage is legal escalation once the case-file route is exhausted. A consulting firm's leverage is ex-Amazon staff who have seen the pattern before. A retainer shop's leverage is that reinstatement is bundled with everything else you already pay them for. Dr. Shield's leverage is that reinstatement, reimbursement, fee disputes and Brand Registry work all run through the same case log, so a suppressed-listing case and the reimbursement claim sitting behind it get argued together rather than by two different vendors who never talk to each other.

Common questions

What is the difference between Amazon Sellers Lawyer and Amazon Sellers Attorney?

They are two different law firms with similar names. Amazon Sellers Lawyer (amazonsellerslawyer.com) is the trading name of Rosenbaum & Segall, P.C. Amazon Sellers Attorney (amazonsellersattorney.com) is a separate firm run by James E. Ranalli in Seattle. A third name, AMZ Sellers Attorney (amazonsellers.attorney), is a third distinct business again. Confirm the exact domain before you act on any review or testimonial for either.

Should I hire a lawyer or a reinstatement service for a suspended Amazon account?

It depends on where you are in the process. Most suspensions are resolved through Amazon's own appeal channel, which a consulting firm or a case-management service handles daily. A lawyer's leverage — demand letters, arbitration under the Business Solutions Agreement — matters once that route is exhausted or when the case involves an intellectual-property dispute or a legal claim against you. Several services, including us, refer a case to a lawyer when it has reached that point rather than keep arguing it inside the appeal system.

How long does Amazon account reinstatement take?

It varies by case type and cannot be honestly promised in advance. A straightforward policy-violation appeal can clear in days; a suppressed-listing case with multiple escalations has taken 40 to 45 days in our own casework. What predicts the timeline more than the vendor is case discipline — one case ID rather than duplicates, calling every three days, and choosing internal review over re-appealing, since a fresh appeal restarts the clock.

Can any service guarantee my Amazon account will be reinstated?

No, and any that implies it should be a warning sign. Amazon decides every case; a reinstatement service argues it with evidence and process. Amazon's own policy also warns that premature, duplicate or insufficiently researched requests can trigger "monitoring, investigation, and account action" — the opposite of what a rushed guarantee-driven approach would produce.
