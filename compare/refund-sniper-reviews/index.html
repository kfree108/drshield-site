What happened to Refund Sniper

Checked 21 August 2026: refundsniper.com 301-redirects to dimetydsellers.com. Refund Sniper was one of three previously independent FBA reimbursement brands — alongside Seller Locker and SellerBench — that Threecolts has folded into one consolidated product, DimeTyd Sellers. There is no separate Refund Sniper pricing page, no separate signup, and no separate support line anymore. Search results and older listicles still carrying a live-looking "Refund Sniper" entry are reading a stale index of a site that no longer functions as its own business.

What Refund Sniper was known for, and what survives it

Refund Sniper positioned itself around speed and automated claim detection — auditing an account's transaction history and filing reimbursement claims quickly once eligibility windows opened, competing on turnaround time as much as recovery percentage. That positioning survives inside DimeTyd Sellers' current offering: a weekly invoice cycle for 25% of what we recover, covering "50+ reimbursement scenarios," with a dedicated case manager assigned per account. If speed of detection was the reason you were originally drawn to Refund Sniper, that mechanism is still present in the merged product — it is just no longer sold under that name or evaluated on its own.

Why three brands became one, and what it means for reviews

Seller Locker, SellerBench and Refund Sniper all now 301-redirect into the DimeTyd Sellers property, and the DimeTyd Sellers footer reads "3CG 2025 SellerBench LLC" — the same corporate entity behind all three. Threecolts' own company-wide Trustpilot record shows a 4.4 TrustScore from 67 reviews, read 21 August 2026 — but that figure covers threecolts.com broadly, spanning its whole suite of seller tools, not the DimeTyd Sellers reimbursement product specifically. There is no independently verifiable product-level rating for the merged reimbursement service as of this reading, and treating the company-wide score as a stand-in for it would overstate what is actually known.

What to check before assuming Refund Sniper's old terms still apply

Any pricing, turnaround-time claim, or feature list you find for "Refund Sniper" predates the merger and should be treated as historical rather than current. Read DimeTyd Sellers' own pricing page directly rather than relying on a cached description of the brand it absorbed. The current published rate — 25% of what is recovered, weekly invoicing — is worth comparing against TrueOps' lower published 10% commission and against GETIDA's "starting at 25%," both live, standalone competitors with their own current pricing pages as of 21 August 2026.

What a real reimbursement figure looks like once you account for reversals

Whichever brand name a vendor now trades under, any recovery total worth trusting should be stated net of reversals, not gross. Amazon's own policy reserves the right to reverse a reimbursement "if a reimbursement was made in error, or if a reimbursed item is later found and returned to your inventory." Across our own managed accounts since 10 February 2026, reimbursement recovery totals $803,457 net of $170,047 in reversals — roughly 17% of the gross figure clawed back — which is the honest shape of what any reimbursement vendor's headline number should look like once reversals are accounted for. A vendor quoting only a gross recovery figure, merged brand or standalone, is quoting the more flattering number rather than the more accurate one.

What the merged product still does not cover

Like every reimbursement-only vendor in this category, DimeTyd Sellers is scoped to money Amazon owes for lost, damaged or mishandled inventory. Its FBA Fee Protector add-on is the one meaningful exception — comparing SKU measurements against Amazon's own fee report daily and requesting corrections, which is the closest any competitor in this space comes to dimension-dispute work. What remains untouched: suppressed listings, variation and Brand Registry attacks, and account-health cases. Amazon's own reimbursement policy also caps single-unit reimbursements at $5,000 and values pre-order losses at sourcing cost rather than sale price since 31 March 2025 — a floor that applies to any vendor's recovery figures, merged brand or not.

How to confirm a redirect like this is legitimate, not a security concern

A 301 redirect from a familiar domain to an unfamiliar one is worth verifying before you enter any account credentials, and the check is simple: the DimeTyd Sellers footer names the same corporate entity — "3CG 2025 SellerBench LLC" — that also operates Threecolts' broader suite, and the redirect target is a stable, actively maintained site with its own current pricing and support pages rather than a placeholder. That is the pattern of a genuine corporate consolidation, not a hijacked domain. The more useful caution is business rather than security: read the current terms on the destination site directly rather than assuming anything from the old Refund Sniper pages still applies, since pricing, claim scope and support structure can all have changed in the merge.

Both are fair, specific questions a vendor confident in its post-merger service should answer plainly.

Common questions

Does Refund Sniper still exist?

Not as a standalone service. refundsniper.com now redirects to dimetydsellers.com, Threecolts' consolidated reimbursement product, which also absorbed Seller Locker and SellerBench. There is no separate Refund Sniper pricing, signup, or support anymore.

What replaced Refund Sniper?

DimeTyd Sellers, which invoices weekly for 25% of what it recovers across more than 50 reimbursement scenarios, with a dedicated case manager per account and an FBA Fee Protector add-on for dimension and fee disputes.

Is DimeTyd Sellers' 4.4-star rating specific to the reimbursement product?

No — that Trustpilot score, from 67 reviews read 21 August 2026, covers threecolts.com company-wide across its whole suite of seller tools, not the DimeTyd Sellers reimbursement product specifically. There is no verified product-level rating available.

Should I trust older reviews or pricing pages for Refund Sniper?

No — treat them as historical. Anything describing Refund Sniper's independent pricing, turnaround times, or features predates the merger. Read DimeTyd Sellers' current pricing page directly for what actually applies today.

Does the merged DimeTyd Sellers product cover fee disputes?

Partially — its FBA Fee Protector add-on compares SKU measurements against Amazon's own fee report daily, the closest any pure reimbursement vendor in this category comes to dimension-dispute work. It still does not cover suppressed listings, variation attacks, or Brand Registry casework.

Should a recovery total be quoted gross or net of reversals?

Net — always. Amazon reserves the right to reverse a reimbursement made in error or when a lost item is later found, and a headline figure that ignores that overstates what actually landed. Our own $803,457 figure is net of $170,047 in reversals; ask any vendor, merged brand or standalone, whether their published totals are calculated the same way.
